      Ground rules for option buyers

      Before the open

      • Write down your trigger, target and stop from the plan above before 9:15. Don't decide in the heat of the move.
      • Check the global cues: GIFT Nifty, US markets overnight, and any big news or results due.
      • Decide your size first. Risk no more than 1–2% of your capital on one trade.

      During the session

      • Skip the first 15 minutes. Opening moves often reverse.
      • Wait for a 15-minute candle to close beyond a level. A quick poke through it often fails.
      • Use ATM or one strike in-the-money for direction trades. Cheap far-OTM options rarely pay off.
      • Set your stop on the Nifty level, not on the option price. Premiums swing too much to be a reliable stop.

      Protecting capital

      • Never add to a losing option position. Time decay works against you every hour.
      • Book part of the profit at the first target and trail the rest.
      • On expiry day, avoid holding bought options after 1:30 PM unless the trade is well in profit.
      • After two losing trades in a day, stop for the day.

      What the numbers mean

      Pivot, R1, R2, S1, S2
      Levels calculated from the last session's high, low and close. R means resistance above the price, S means support below it.
      Open interest (OI)
      The number of option contracts still open at a strike. Large OI shows where option sellers have placed their bets.
      Put-call ratio (PCR)
      Total put OI divided by total call OI. Above 1.3 usually means heavy put selling (supportive); below 0.7 means heavy call selling (bearish).
      Max pain
      The strike where option buyers as a whole would lose the most at expiry. Nifty often drifts toward it near expiry.
      India VIX
      The market's expected volatility over the next 30 days. Higher VIX means bigger expected moves and more expensive options.
      ATR
      Average true range: how many points Nifty has typically moved in a day recently.

      Option chain and volatility

      Large call OI above the price often acts as resistance. Large put OI below it often acts as support.

      India VIX, following the chart range on the Dashboard tab

      Above 20 means high fear and expensive premiums. Below 12 means a calm market and cheap premiums.

      How past signals played out

      Each signal is graded as a one-day plan on the next session: entry at R1 for calls or S1 for puts, target R2 or S2, and stop at the pivot. Points are Nifty index points, not option premium. Daily candles don't show which level was hit first, so a day that touched both the stop and the target counts as a loss. Rows marked "bf" were backfilled on the first run using price-based rules only.

      This is an educational tool, not investment advice. Signals come from simple rules and are often wrong. Buying options can lose the whole premium quickly, and SEBI's study found most individual F&O traders lose money. Option-chain data comes from NSE and can be missing or delayed.
